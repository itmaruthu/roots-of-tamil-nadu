A friend in your own village. Someone who knows the temple, the old streets and the elders, who finds what your family left behind and welcomes you when you come home.

Find my village Become an Ooru Nanbar

Roots is a prototype. The Ooru Nanbar network begins with the Madurai pilot, and this page shows how it will work.

  • Before you comeFinds your village, the family temple and the old house, and sends you photos and an elder's voice.
  • When you comeMeets you in the village, arranges the pongal or abhishekam, introduces the elders and stays with you for the day.
  • Between visitsOffers a pooja in your name with a video, looks in on elderly relatives and keeps an eye on the old house.

Before any trip

We found your village

Many families know only a village name and a grandfather's name. Tell us what you remember. An Ooru Nanbar goes there and sends back what they find, whether or not you ever travel.

What the report holds

  • Photos of the family temple, the street and the old house, if it still stands
  • A voice note from an elder who remembers the family
  • The exact spot on a map
  • The temple's festival days, and the best dates to come
  • A plain answer if nothing could be found

Trust

Five checks before anyone becomes an Ooru Nanbar

  1. Identity and addressA government photo ID, and proof of living in the village.
  2. Someone vouchesThe panchayat president, the temple trustee or the school head puts their name to it.
  3. Police verificationA verification certificate, renewed regularly.
  4. A visit in personA Roots coordinator meets them at home and walks the village with them.
  5. Bank name match and trainingPayment goes only to an account in their own name, after a short course in Tamil on welcome and safety.

Safety and money

Safe for the family, fair to the village

  • You pay Roots, not cash in hand

    The Ooru Nanbar is paid by UPI after you confirm the visit.

  • Someone always knows where you are

    Your plan is shared with a relative, with check-ins through the day.

  • We never ask your caste

    You name your village and your temple. The match follows the place.

  • A coordinator who speaks English

    Each district has a trained Roots coordinator who looks after about twenty villages and translates for the children.

Planned pilot rates, still to be confirmed: ₹2,200 for a day with a family, ₹700 for a village report, ₹400 for a pooja or a visit to an elder.

Who makes a good Ooru Nanbar

  • The priest or trustee of a village temple
  • A retired teacher
  • A leader of a women's self-help group
  • Anyone the village trusts and who knows its families

    Questions people ask

    Why not just call our relatives?

    If you have relatives in the village, do. Roots is for families who have lost touch, and for the planning relatives cannot do from afar.

    What if you cannot find our village?

    We tell you plainly what we looked for and what we found. Nobody is sent to a village on a guess.

    Is the Ooru Nanbar a tourist guide?

    No. A village host who knows the place and its people. Monuments stay with licensed guides.

    What happens after the first visit?

    Roots keeps your family's village record, and your Ooru Nanbar can offer a pooja or look in on relatives between visits.

    Who do we turn to if something goes wrong?

    To Roots. Every Ooru Nanbar is checked, every visit is recorded, and a coordinator is a phone call away.

    How is my family's information kept?

    Only you, your Ooru Nanbar and the Roots coordinator see it. An elder's voice is recorded only with their consent.
